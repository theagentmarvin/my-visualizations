The FDE thesis

Stop renting generic AI.
Own your Estimating Model Factory.

In 2023, "Constructora Andes" (Santiago, CLP ~950/USD) priced 40 bids a year from a fortress of 12 Excel masters, 4 shared drives, and one senior estimator — "Don Patricio" — whose 30 years of gut feel lived only in his head. A junior estimator needed 11 days to produce a single BOQ, re-keying quantities from Procore takeoffs, then hand-copying unit rates from a 2019 cost catalog. Estimates were wrong in both directions: the firm won jobs it shouldn't (losing 6–14% at closeout) and lost jobs it should have won (pricing 8% above the winning bid). Nobody could say why, because the reasons were locked in the spreadsheet cells and in Patricio's memory. The FDE's thesis: stop renting generic AI to read those spreadsheets — build a compounding Estimating Model Factory that owns the firm's specialized pricing intelligence. Each estimate becomes an Episode (context + action + measured outcome); each estimator's override becomes a Gate that emits preference data; each closed project feeds actual-cost variance back as a label. After 90 days, the factory prices bids in 3 days instead of 11, and every month of operation makes the next estimate smarter. This is the artifact's arc: from spreadsheet chaos → ontology → episodes → agents → a self-improving loop the firm owns.

Days 0–30 Ontology wiring & episode extraction backfill

      
Ingest ERP + Procore + cost-catalog into the ontology. Backfill 120 past estimates → Episodes with Measured Outcome labels.

      

        
FrictionDirty historical data — missing unit rates, mismatched BOQ item codes, estimates stored as PDFs not rows.

        
FDE moveShip a dataQualityScore per episode; only episodes > 0.6 enter the mixture; document the data-quality debt in a one-pager the CFO reads.

        
FrictionEstimators fear replacement.

        
FDE move"Your assistant, not your replacement" — the first agent is gated by estimators; their overrides are the highest-value input. Enlist "Don Patricio" as co-designer of the rate-check agent (his 28 years become the SFT gold set).

      

    

    

      
Days 31–60 First agent behind a gate

      
Deploy the Takeoff Agent on IFC auto-quantities only, behind a hard human approval gate. Start Post-Mortem Agent on 10 closed projects.

      

        
FrictionEstimators "don't trust the numbers" and double-check everything (override rate spikes).

        
FDE moveShow a side-by-side diff (agent vs estimator) with a confidence badge; celebrate that overrides are training the model, not failures.

      

    

    

      
Days 61–90 Model factory v1 & compounding loop

      
Stand up Model Factory v1: nightly mixture rebuild + LoRA fine-tune + offline A/B eval. Promote the first model. Turn on Rate-Check & Quote Normalizer; Bid Risk in advisory mode.

      

        
FrictionOps team resists a new system during live bid season.

        
FDE moveRun the factory in parallel (shadow mode) — the model prices bids it doesn't influence — and report the divergence. Only after 30 days of shadow-mode green KPIs does the factory go live.
