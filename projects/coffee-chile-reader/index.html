Coffee Production in Chile: History, Economics, Regions, and Volumes

Executive Summary

Chile has virtually no commercial coffee production due to unfavorable climate conditions across its continental territory. The only notable efforts are small-scale, artisanal cultivation on Easter Island (Rapa Nui), where wild coffee plants introduced in the early 19th century persist. Production volumes are negligible compared to global leaders, with Chile absent from FAO lists of significant producers. Economic impact is minimal, though specialty coffee from Rapa Nui holds niche potential.

Key Takeaways:

  • No large-scale production; climate unsuitable for continental Chile.
  • Easter Island hosts wild and small artisanal plantations targeting specialty market.
  • Chile is a net importer of coffee for domestic consumption.

Date: 2026-02-15 | Researcher: Wiki-Marvin Subagent

History

Coffee plants were likely introduced to Easter Island (Rapa Nui) by Dutch navigators in the early 19th century. These plants have survived as wild specimens scattered across the island.[s1][s2]

In 2017, initiatives led by local importers and agronomists aimed to revive and commercialize Rapa Nui coffee as a specialty product, focusing on organic, artisanal production rather than volume.[s2] Continental Chile has historically produced coffee substitutes (e.g., barley coffee) due to lack of suitable tropical climates.[s1]

No evidence of significant historical production on the mainland.

Regions

Easter Island (Rapa Nui)

  • The sole region with viable coffee plants.
  • Subtropical climate allows survival of wild Coffea species.
  • Small family plots and experimental plantations.[s1][s2][s4]

Continental Chile

Unsuitable:

  • Northern regions (e.g., Atacama Desert): Extreme aridity blocks humidity.[s5]
  • Central/Southern regions: Temperatures too low for coffee arabica/robusta.

Production Volumes

Chile does not appear in FAO's list of 36 countries producing >10,000 tonnes of green coffee in 2023 (world total: 11.1 million tonnes).[s3]

Economics

  • Production Impact: Negligible contribution to GDP or exports.
  • Consumption/Imports: Chile imports significant volumes for domestic market (e.g., Statista data on green coffee imports).[s7]
  • Niche Opportunity: Rapa Nui coffee as premium specialty product, leveraging uniqueness for tourism and high-end markets.[s2][s4]
  • Global Context: Coffee belt excludes most of Chile geographically.
