Andrés Niño's Journey in El Salvador during the Spanish Conquest Era

c. 1476–1525 — The First European to Explore the Pacific Coast

Research Report v2 2026-02-16

Summary

Andrés Niño (c. 1476–1525), a Spanish pilot from Moguer, was the first European to explore the Pacific coast of what is now El Salvador in 1522 as part of Gil González Dávila's expedition from Panama.

His journey marked the "discovery" of key geographical features, including the Gulf of Fonseca, Meanguera Island (named Petronila), Jiquilisco Bay, and the Lempa River mouth [src1][src2][src3]. Niño's coastal reconnaissance preceded Pedro de Alvarado's inland conquest of the Pipil lordship of Cuzcatlán (1524–1528), facilitating Spanish claims in the region.

Limited by sources, Niño's role was exploratory rather than militaristic; he died during later expeditions in Honduras [src3].

Timeline

1519

Niño captains expedition with Gil González Dávila along Pacific coast from Panama to Nicaragua [src3].

January/May 1522

Expedition sails from Panama; Niño separates at Nicoya Peninsula (Costa Rica), heads northwest [src1][src2][src3].

~31 May 1522

Discovers and lands on Meanguera Island (Gulf of Fonseca), names it Petronila; names gulf after Juan Rodríguez de Fonseca [src1][src2][src3].

June 1522

Explores Jiquilisco Bay and Lempa River mouth (El Salvador coast) [src1][src2].

1522

Continues north to Gulf of Tehuantepec (Mexico), returns south [src3].

1523

Returns to Panama with gold and pearls [src3].

1524

Joins González Dávila's Nicaragua expedition; Alvarado enters El Salvador interior via Río Paz [src1][src2].

1525

Niño dies in Honduras during expedition [src3].

1524–1528

Alvarado conquers Cuzcatlán (Pipil heartland); San Salvador founded 1525/1528 [src1][src2].

Historical Context

Pre-conquest El Salvador featured Pipil (Nahua-related) in Cuzcatlán (central-west), Lenca (east), Xinca, Maya groups (Ch'orti', Poqomam). Population ~130k–1M; weakened by 1520 smallpox [src1][src2].

Niño's 1522 voyage was amid rival Spanish thrusts: from Panama (González), Mexico (Alvarado). Coastal focus complemented Alvarado's highland campaigns. Legacy: Enabled encomiendas, San Salvador (1525), integration into Guatemala Captaincy General.

Source Gaps: No Niño portrait; primary accounts sparse (Alvarado letters, Oviedo) [src1][src3].
