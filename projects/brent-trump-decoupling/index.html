🔍 The Decoupling: Is It Real?

Yes — and it evolved in three distinct phases:

Phase 1 — Pre-war (Jan–Feb): Trump tweets = predictable, large moves. Tariffs, energy orders, and rhetoric moved Brent 3–8% within hours. Markets were calibrated to a "normal" political risk environment.

Phase 2 — Acute war shock (Feb 28 – Mar 15): The war itself dominates. Tweet effect is BINARY and SHORT-LIVED. Every threat sends oil up $5–10; every peace signal sends it down $10–15 — but both reverses happen within hours/days as physical reality reasserts. The market is essentially trading the war timeline, not the tweet.

Phase 3 — Fatigue + structural binding (Mar 23 onward): This is where the decoupling you're observing emerges. The market has learned: (a) Trump's peace tweets don't mean Hormuz reopens, (b) his threats don't mean further escalation, (c) the physical supply crunch is the only thing that matters. The NYT correctly identified this — peace signals from Trump now barely move prices because they were proven FALSE within 48 hours THREE times.

📉 Why "Good News" Tweets Drop Prices More Than Expected

When Trump tweets peace/pause → market ANTICIPATES Hormuz reopening → massive short-covering + short-selling by traders who've been burned buying the dip and getting trapped. On March 23, Brent dropped $17 (15%) in hours because the pre-positioned longs were FINALLY given an exit. On April 17, the Iran-ceasefire news dropped Brent $10 in one session — even though the US blockade is still in place. The "good" news triggered mass algorithmic de-risking.

The information asymmetry problem: Traders know something markets don't — they track actual tanker flows (via Kpler, etc.). They KNOW only 2–24 ships/day are passing Hormuz vs. the normal 300+. So when Trump says "talks are going well," a trader thinks: "He's wrong, or he's lying, because I can see 0 tankers clearing." The tweet is priced against physical reality, not sentiment.

📈 Why "Bad News" Tweets Don't Rise As Much As Expected

When Trump threatens to bomb Iran, the market already KNOWS there's a war. The risk premium was set at $40–50/bbl above fair value the moment Hormuz closed on Feb 28. Additional threats don't ADD new information — they're noise on top of a known structural supply disruption. Moreover:

(1) Traders have been burned buying the spike — every time bad news sent oil up $8–15, it reversed within days when the threatened action didn't materialize. The April 7 "whole civilization will die" tweet moved Brent by approximately NOTHING. The threat fatigue is real — a fourth empty threat carries zero credibility.

(2) Structural supply constraints are priced in — even if Iran re-closes Hormuz, the market has already repriced to $95–130 range. You can't shock a market twice with the same information.

(3) Blockade ≠ shooting war — when Trump posted about destroying Iranian fast attack craft on April 13, Brent actually FELL initially because traders interpreted the blockade (not shooting) as a managed escalation, not a sign things are getting worse.
